We packed up our panniers soon after the meal and decided to ride the alternate levee route that Joe described to us last night. It had rained overnight and was still drizzling. Luckily, the road was mostly flat.

*Yes to the flat road!*

When we were taking a water break on the side of the road, a pickup truck from the other side stopped next to us. The driver was an 84 year old man named Willard. At first, he asked about our trip and where we are going, then the conversation turned sour when he told us that Jesus is the only way and Islam is a fake religion and Muslims shouldn’t be in the US. I wasn’t going to debate with him on the side of the road, but if we were in a restaurant sitting side by side, like the time I chatted with Pastor Jim in Boonville, KY, then I would have. This time Crispin stepped in and told him that we are all the same if we strip down to the basic principles of these different religions. I thought we were going to be here for a while then another pickup truck stopped in between us. We took the opportunity to get on our way.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhiclfipFx3bvKFY9KB0UWITokNoeq7O-oKOmCbA3IqjKvaL-OrFVRjK1D_Q19beFN42hWpSPP_ehpNipTL3TWrYg5LLikAqfBFUA7BWfPrKOlFbzsllKwdGLw1PmZ6flBECagytPZ3bws/w640-h480/%255BUNSET%255D)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhiclfipFx3bvKFY9KB0UWITokNoeq7O-oKOmCbA3IqjKvaL-OrFVRjK1D_Q19beFN42hWpSPP_ehpNipTL3TWrYg5LLikAqfBFUA7BWfPrKOlFbzsllKwdGLw1PmZ6flBECagytPZ3bws/s1600/%255BUNSET%255)
*Willard*

We got to the levee not far down the road. Crispin wanted to ride on top of it. I was hesitant because it’s all gravel and maybe bad for my tires. I rode on it anyway because when will I have another chance to ride a bicycle on a levee. We rode for two miles or so and I didn’t like it at all. It was bumpy with all the rocks. I told Crispin that I’ll surprised if I didn’t get a flat tire. I was so glad when we got off the levee and onto a somewhat paved road.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjsmjKH4EsaeN7NBOU4eqWX91EE7WAZlNiy6muoz5uQ20kIxLL8HGWSX0itpqddny3LHnUIOfCItDP464yz-Slulbra7AbfLobnGoGU7A9ZiELCRGCHwLmAKXNjCaK28hyphenhyphenaxmmndo5ro1c/w640-h480/%255BUNSET%255D)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjsmjKH4EsaeN7NBOU4eqWX91EE7WAZlNiy6muoz5uQ20kIxLL8HGWSX0itpqddny3LHnUIOfCItDP464yz-Slulbra7AbfLobnGoGU7A9ZiELCRGCHwLmAKXNjCaK28hyphenhyphenaxmmndo5ro1c/s1600/%255BUNSET%255)
*Riding on the levee*

Then there was the coal facility in the small town of Cora. We saw some kind of a conveyor belt that looked like a bridge going over the road. Not far down was a mountain of coal. Our Warmshowers host, Joe, had told us the coal in Illinois has sulfur in it so it’s a subpar product. Crispin surmised that this coal would probably be sold overseas where the environmental regulation is lax.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjhdKm4kWygen-cpaiXaCpeLUjoVnEOtxH1V4-7MTvgHdWc0NXghJv1O0qAdBbzEiFOfLxvShr45K_CJirzeJuchgTyoySaPmUV3frOaqhdzL8B9E6OiQqXtx9A6uTOnU8lHS31D7SzTDI/w640-h480/%255BUNSET%255D)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjhdKm4kWygen-cpaiXaCpeLUjoVnEOtxH1V4-7MTvgHdWc0NXghJv1O0qAdBbzEiFOfLxvShr45K_CJirzeJuchgTyoySaPmUV3frOaqhdzL8B9E6OiQqXtx9A6uTOnU8lHS31D7SzTDI/s1600/%255BUNSET%255)
*Coal truck, and the mountain of coal in the background*

Just as I was passing by the mountain of coal, my rear tire got a flat, as I had predicted. Under the drizzle rain, Crispin and I spent about 20 minutes to take the tire off, take the tube out, replace the tube with a new one, and use my CO2 cartridge to inject air into the tube. The tire wasn’t at its highest pressure, but we had to ride on.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj6FlciV9GR1M1MVSI88Yd8H567maSFQRns6E897aFwH7Aljbmmdxh6F6fQA1HSH6-4-JCy0f2rYwXY2QkP497Y_QK69cQKMAwgBDALMVFOcMnizPAf-vGnGywgfeX_97dvgWacRY98D3U/w640-h480/%255BUNSET%255D)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj6FlciV9GR1M1MVSI88Yd8H567maSFQRns6E897aFwH7Aljbmmdxh6F6fQA1HSH6-4-JCy0f2rYwXY2QkP497Y_QK69cQKMAwgBDALMVFOcMnizPAf-vGnGywgfeX_97dvgWacRY98D3U/s1600/%255BUNSET%255)
*First flat of the trip*

As we got close to Chester, the rain started to pour. It felt like I was taking a shower while riding my bicycle. We had to climb a few hills. One of them was too steep for me so I walked my bike in the downpour while numerous coal trucks passed by me, spraying rain on me. Once I got up the hill, Crispin and I took shelter at a carport in the driveway of a house. We were eating some snacks when an older couple came out of the house. Like most people, they were very nice and allowed us to stay awhile.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgP4cG-L_TVOI3m2TjpTq44YCxrIyRPo8_3mldK3HNC87sCtTo4AnSR352GV8gsj3G6g0If5ESW3ZEzeh1_DRzp83j0LwlctNcFj6W5FVXVZden1EM6OdQHmTH0zbp6ptIUauZ5ctKQj5s/w640-h480/%255BUNSET%255D)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgP4cG-L_TVOI3m2TjpTq44YCxrIyRPo8_3mldK3HNC87sCtTo4AnSR352GV8gsj3G6g0If5ESW3ZEzeh1_DRzp83j0LwlctNcFj6W5FVXVZden1EM6OdQHmTH0zbp6ptIUauZ5ctKQj5s/s1600/%255BUNSET%255)
*Walking my bike uphill in a downpour*

Once the rain eased a little, we rode two more miles to Chester. We ended up riding about 42 miles to Chester from Murphysboro since we had missed a turn earlier in the morning. We had a late lunch at Hardee’s around 2:30 PM then came to the bike hostel at Fraternity of the Eagles bar and restaurant. There we met Frank, a cyclist who is aiming to go 100 miles a day from Yorktown, VA, to San Francisco, CA.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgJOKXKaSQv0L2GW7fepS83Vmh7AQ93gQ9MqDhWcMHkEuQ8lRhpFgNCEDpTTV19UZjoUo-QI4Iixh__obLWUY-WHlKKEc06OiRYXFz13arXGoCQ1HTBcSuGmwTDBgMqOBv8t1cdk8ilw_o/w480-h640/%255BUNSET%255D)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgJOKXKaSQv0L2GW7fepS83Vmh7AQ93gQ9MqDhWcMHkEuQ8lRhpFgNCEDpTTV19UZjoUo-QI4Iixh__obLWUY-WHlKKEc06OiRYXFz13arXGoCQ1HTBcSuGmwTDBgMqOBv8t1cdk8ilw_o/s1600/%255BUNSET%255)
*Frank, the speediest cyclist we have met so far*

After we settled down in the hostel the rain stopped. The three of us had an early dinner at the bar and restaurant. When we came back to the hostel two more cyclists showed up. They are a couple from Boston. While they went to get food, I decided to try and fix my broken tube by patching it, but the hole was too big. I cut a section out to use it later as a tire boot if needed. I went back to the bar and restaurant and found Crispin talking to the new cyclists. Their names are Jill and Zack. We shared our experiences over beer and had a great conversation.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgW9tTX8KQ3vP5ZkazKMARelJrVD47SZsbfzrSq7BQd0zNmAiOjTQy-3fUhTRx9WfIRbwKT1cWa8Xaer6LTP75ITcj0dDD_vMgOzQIVmUIQs4f-ZC1yAPtt6j3RUQVI6Zq1If0XxzHgIkE/w640-h480/%255BUNSET%255D)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgW9tTX8KQ3vP5ZkazKMARelJrVD47SZsbfzrSq7BQd0zNmAiOjTQy-3fUhTRx9WfIRbwKT1cWa8Xaer6LTP75ITcj0dDD_vMgOzQIVmUIQs4f-ZC1yAPtt6j3RUQVI6Zq1If0XxzHgIkE/s1600/%255BUNSET%255)

I’m going to call it a day and will walk back to the hostel. There will be 5 of us total sleeping in a small room tonight. I hope there won’t be too much snoring.
